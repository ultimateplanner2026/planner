2

Your phone keeps old notes — and could overwrite the newer ones

live — needs your approval

Which control: nothing you click — the sync that runs whenever the planner opens, refreshes, or you come back to its tab. You see it in the Notes tab and in a task’s note.

"on the computer for my work I have newer stuff as notes, however it did not update in the browser on my phone … it has to be matching with the phone"

was wrong

On a phone connection the phone uploaded its OLD notes before its own read came back — overwriting the work computer’s newer notes in the cloud — and then was shown its own upload. A fast computer’s read wins that race, which is why the computer updated and the phone never did. Refreshing, switching tabs and signing out each uploaded first.

And even on a fast connection a task note came out as new text ⸻ old text.

now

An upload can never overwrite what this device has not seen: it reads the cloud first, merges note by note (newest edit wins) and writes the merged copy; if the cloud changes in between, the write is refused and merged again.

Reads come from the server, coming back to the tab checks immediately, and a task note shows only its newest text.

4

Moving a task reaches the other device

needs your approval

Which control: the ← and → arrows in a task’s ⋮ menu on the priority tree (Move out one level / Make a subtask of the one above), reordering the tasks in a branch, and Undo after a move.

"go ahead and build it" — the first step of one cloud record per item. Found while designing it: a move made on one device never reached the other.

was wrong

A move only reached the other device if the task was also edited. The sync compared what a task says, not where it sits — so indenting, outdenting or reordering on the laptop left the phone with its old arrangement, and a rename on the phone could quietly undo the laptop’s move.

now

A move is recorded with its own time, separate from edits. The other device takes the newest move and the newest edit independently, so a rename and a move made at the same moment both survive. Two tasks moved under each other at once end in one arrangement on both devices, and neither is lost.
